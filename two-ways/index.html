    How to read the phonetics

    Every Russian or French line has a second line underneath: a reading guide written with the reader's own habits. It's an approximation — listen to your partner, and use it as a crutch, not a rule.

    Russian, read the French way

    For the French speaker.

    CAPS
    the stressed syllable — longer and louder: spa-SSI-ba
    a
    unstressed о sounds like "a" — that's why молоко is ma-la-KO
    t, s, k…
    every final consonant is pronounced: pri-VIÉT, khliép
    nn, mm
    never nasal: a clear vowel, then n/m — a-DINN
    ou · ch · j
    у · ш · ж
    tch · chtch
    ч · щ (a long, soft "ch")
    kh
    х — like the Spanish jota
    y
    ы — say "i" with the tongue pulled back
    '
    soft consonant — a tiny "y" glide after it: pit'
    ï
    й — as in "aïe": tchaï
    ss
    always a hard s, never z

    French, read the Russian way

    For the Russian speaker.

    ´
    French stresses the last syllable of a phrase, lightly; keep the rest even
    он ан эн
    н at the end of a syllable = nasal vowel: don't close on the н, let the vowel ring through the nose — бонжу́р
    нн
    a real, clear n — бонн (bonne)
    ю
    French u: say "и" with lips rounded as for "у" — тю
    ё
    French eu / e: "э" with rounded lips, no й before it — дё
    р
    French r — soft, from the back of the throat
    е / э
    е = closed é (don't soften the consonant much), э = open è
    silent
    silent letters are already removed — read only what you see
